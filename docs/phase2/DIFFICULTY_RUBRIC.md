# Difficulty rubric

Difficulty has two recorded dimensions: a five-level curricular progression and a delivery mode. Numeric `difficulty` values map to the level names below; the mapping is held in central policy configuration.

| Level | Name | Primary demand |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Foundations | Recall or recognise one explicitly taught fact or definition |
| 2 | Distinctions | Discriminate related concepts using relevant vocabulary |
| 3 | Evidence | Identify, interpret or apply relevant Scripture, doctrine or historical evidence |
| 4 | Objections | Analyse a serious objection and the Catholic response without caricature |
| 5 | Synthesis | Integrate several doctrines, sources or argumentative steps with precise scope |

The delivery mode is `easy`, `medium`, `hard`, `expert`, or `trick`.

- **Easy:** one taught fact; plausible misconception distractors.
- **Medium:** a distinction among related concepts; all options use relevant language.
- **Hard:** a scenario or source interpretation; distractors may be partial truths.
- **Expert:** synthesis across sources or doctrines; options differ by authority, implication or scope.
- **Trick:** exposes an approved category error or equivocation; never hidden context or misleading grammar.

A level and mode combination must be pedagogically defensible. A Foundations/Expert item is exceptional and requires an assessment-review note. Reading difficulty, obscure vocabulary, time pressure and deceptive phrasing do not raise valid difficulty.

## Calibration checklist

Reviewers ask:

1. Was the assessed knowledge taught?
2. Does the level match the objective?
3. Is the correct answer uniquely best under the stated scope?
4. Are distractors conceptually close for the claimed mode?
5. Does any option-length, grammar or position clue reveal the answer?
6. Could a well-formed learner explain the decisive distinction?
7. For Expert or Trick, has an independent assessment reviewer approved the item?

Analytics may recommend recalibration or quarantine but never retroactively relock completed content.
