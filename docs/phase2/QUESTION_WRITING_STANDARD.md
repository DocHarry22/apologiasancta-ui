# Multiple-choice question-writing standard

All Apologia Sancta quiz items are multiple-choice. The default and official delivery format has four enabled options and exactly one best answer.

## Required record

A question must have:

- a clear, self-contained stem;
- one linked learning objective;
- level (Foundations through Synthesis) and mode (Easy through Trick);
- exactly four enabled options, unless a separately approved accessibility/interface exception is recorded;
- one best answer;
- a correct-answer explanation;
- an explanation for every option;
- a misconception code for every distractor;
- at least one authoritative source with a locator;
- named comparative tradition and scope where applicable;
- translation, copyright, permission, licence and attribution metadata;
- author, current-version reviewers, version and publication status;
- equivalence key for retake selection; and
- quarantine state/reason when validity is in doubt.

## Stem rules

The stem tests the linked objective rather than general cleverness. It supplies all context needed to answer. Avoid ambiguous pronouns, hidden assumptions, unnecessary negatives and double negatives. If a source is interpreted, provide the relevant excerpt or locator within permission limits.

## Option rules

Distractors must be plausible because they express a recognisable misconception, incomplete distinction or misapplication. They may not be jokes, absurdities, straw men, grammatical completions available only for one option, or conspicuously shorter than the correct answer. Option order must not encode the answer.

`All of the above` and `None of the above` are prohibited. Several answers may contain partial truth only when the stem makes the one-best-answer criterion explicit and the explanations identify the decisive distinction.

## Explanations

The correct explanation states why the answer is best and connects back to the objective and authoritative source. Each distractor explanation identifies the precise error without insulting the learner or the tradition represented. Explanations are revealed only after an official attempt is submitted.

## Comparative questions

A question names the tradition actually assessed. Advanced comparative items require a steelman statement and an official confession, catechism, recognised institutional document or credible specialist source. “Protestants believe” and “Muslims believe” are invalid scopes.

## Anti-trivia rule

Dates, names and isolated facts are assessable only when the objective makes them educationally meaningful. Recall disconnected from doctrinal, historical, interpretive or apologetic understanding does not establish mastery.

## Quarantine

Any item with ambiguous wording, competing best answers, insufficient source support, licensing uncertainty, a broken reference, a misleading trick, or material reviewer disagreement is quarantined. Quarantine removes future delivery while preserving versioned attempt history.
