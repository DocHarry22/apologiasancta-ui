# Source and licensing policy

Source authority and reuse permission are separate questions. A highly authoritative source may still be copyrighted, and a public web page is not automatically reusable.

## Authority categories

`sacred_scripture`, `sacred_tradition`, `ecumenical_council`, `papal_magisterium`, `dicastery_magisterium`, `catechism`, `canon_law`, `church_father`, `church_doctor`, `official_comparative_source`, `primary_historical_source`, `academic_secondary_source`, `credible_reference`, or `unverified`.

The category records what the source is. It does not pre-decide the classification of every proposition taken from it.

## Required rights metadata

Each source or quotation records:

- canonical title, author/issuing authority, publisher and stable locator;
- URL and separately reviewed domain;
- authority category;
- original language and displayed translation;
- translation name, edition and translator/rightsholder;
- copyright status;
- permission status and evidence;
- licence identifier/terms;
- maximum quotation words or other applicable limit;
- required attribution text;
- commercial, AI/ML, offline, audio and derivative-use flags;
- permission expiry or next review date; and
- reviewer and review timestamp.

Allowed permission states are `unverified`, `public_domain`, `licensed`, `permission_not_required_under_recorded_terms`, `permission_requested`, `denied`, and `expired`. Publication of quoted text is blocked for unverified, requested, denied or expired permission.

## Citation format

- Scripture: translation abbreviation, book chapter:verse; translation/rights record attached.
- Catechism: *Catechism of the Catholic Church*, paragraph(s).
- Council/papal/dicastery: official title, issuing authority, date, paragraph/canon.
- Father/Doctor: author, work, book/chapter/section, critical or recognised edition.
- Historical/academic: author, title, editor/translator when relevant, publication data, page/section and stable identifier.

Direct quotations preserve the text and carry a locator. Paraphrases, interpretations and inferences are explicitly labelled.

## Default-deny quotation rule

Until permission is verified, the platform stores the citation and a staff-only note, not a substantial quotation. The system-wide fallback quotation limit is zero words. A source-specific reviewed limit may raise it. Large passages are never imported merely because they are readable online.

Rights-holder policies demonstrate why this is necessary:

- [USCCB NABRE permissions](https://www.usccb.org/offices/new-american-bible/permissions) distinguish print/eBook and digital-app use and require licensing for digital applications.
- [Crossway ESV permissions](https://www.crossway.org/permissions/) impose edition, quantity, proportion and notice conditions.
- [Biblica permissions](https://www.biblica.com/permissions/) impose quantity, proportion, platform and AI/ML conditions.
- [Vatican legal notes](https://www.vatican.va/content/vatican/en/legal-notes.html) do not grant unrestricted republication merely because official documents are online.
- [Canon 825](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cod-iuris-canonici/eng/documents/cic_lib3-cann822-833_en.html) concerns ecclesial approval of Scripture editions and translations; that approval is not a copyright licence.

## Approved domains

The approved-domain registry supports source-quality and anti-phishing review. Domain approval never grants copyright permission. Subdomains, redirects and mirrors are evaluated explicitly. A prohibited-use flag overrides domain approval.

## Review cadence

Permission evidence is reviewed before first publication, at its recorded expiry, when product monetisation/distribution changes, when AI/ML use changes, or when a rights-holder changes terms. Automated link checks may create warnings but cannot approve rights.
